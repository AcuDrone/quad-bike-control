## 8. Field tuning (each vehicle, web page only; record the final values in the bench notes)
Start values are the vehicle's defaults, the same for every limit (design D14): **buggy** Kp 1.0, Ki 0.35, start throttle limit 4; **quad bike** Kp 3.0, Ki 1.0, start throttle limit 10; acc 1.0, dec 2.5 on both. Check on the card that these are the values in use (a board with earlier saved values keeps them). Tune at the **10 km/h** limit first — it is the hardest — then only check 20 and 30 km/h. Change one value at a time, at most five runs per value.
- [ ] 8.1 Preparation and plant data (governor OFF): flat, open ground; speed sensor calibrated (Speed sensor card; note pulses/rev and circumference); note the throttle calibration idle / full µs and the servo model; on the buggy use L. With direct throttle note (a) the throttle % at which the vehicle starts to creep, (b) the steady throttle % at 10, 20 and 30 km/h, (c) from a steady 10 km/h, a +1 % throttle step: the delay until the speed starts to rise and the time to the new steady speed. Also note the stored throttle calibration (idle / full µs; the buggy is expected to be mirrored, about 2200 → 800). These replace the "assumed" figures in design D13 (K, θ, τ) for that vehicle — on the quad (b) is the first real number for its plant gain; send them to the developer before changing the defaults in code.
- [ ] 8.2 Launch: `SPEED_MAX` 10 km/h (2.78 m/s) from the GCS; governor ON; Diagnostics "Speed" card open (setpoint, speed, throttle). From rest, stick to 100 % and hold. Does not move within ≈ 2 s on a slope: raise the start throttle limit by 1 % at a time until it moves off; lurches when the clutch takes up: lower it by 1 %. On the flat the limit should not matter (model: the buggy moves at ≈ 1–2 %, the quad at ≈ 3–5 %).
- [ ] 8.3 Hold at 10 km/h, 30 s:
  - **Hunts** — speed swings ±1 km/h or more with a period of ≈ 2–4 s and the throttle row swings ±1 % or more (on the quad a throttle flicker of ± 0.5 % with a calm speed is normal, design D15): lower Kp by about 20 % and set Ki to Kp / 3 (buggy 1.0 / 0.35 → 0.8 / 0.27 → 0.65 / 0.22; quad 3.0 / 1.0 → 2.4 / 0.8 → 1.9 / 0.65); never raise the output rate to cure it.
  - **Slow swing** — period ≥ 5 s, throttle moving in single steps of ≈ 0.3 % (or the servo visibly moving only every few steps): this is the resolution limit (design D16); lower Ki by 0.1 to slow it, and note it — a linkage using more servo travel is the fix, not a gain.
  - **Sluggish** — no swing, but a 1 km/h gap takes > 8 s to close or a small slope drops the speed by > 5 km/h: raise Kp by about 20 % with Ki = Kp / 3 (buggy 1.0 → 1.2 / 0.4; quad 3.0 → 3.6 / 1.2); at the first sign of hunting go back one step (that keeps a gain margin of ≈ 2).
- [ ] 8.4 Steps: stick 30 % → 100 % → 30 % at 10 km/h. Overshoot of more than ≈ 1.5 km/h on the way up: lower the acceleration by 0.2 m/s²; throttle closing too abruptly on the way down: lower the deceleration by 0.5 m/s².
- [ ] 8.5 Hill: up a slope with the stick constant — expect a dip of a few km/h that recovers within ≈ 10 s (design D13); down a slope — the at-idle badge appears and the operator brakes (no braking from the governor is expected).
- [ ] 8.6 20 and 30 km/h with the 10 km/h values: launch and hold as in 8.2–8.3. Expect the same smoothness and a slower recovery on hills. If 20 or 30 km/h hunts while 10 km/h does not, the plant gain rises with speed (contrary to D14) — lower Kp until all three are calm and report it. Do not raise gains for 30 km/h if that makes 10 km/h hunt; the 10 km/h tune wins.
- [ ] 8.7 Buggy only: repeat 8.2–8.6 in L (and once in H at 10 km/h, to see the clutch take-up region; the hold there may be rougher — that is expected) and in R (R ceiling 20 km/h; full stick in R = 20 km/h).
- [ ] 8.8 Each vehicle: record the final values. If they differ from that vehicle's defaults by more than about a factor 1.5 (or the start throttle limit by more than 2 %), a follow-up changes the defaults in that branch's `Constants.h` only (`master` for the quad and `old-board`, `aztec` for the buggy) — never the other vehicle's.
